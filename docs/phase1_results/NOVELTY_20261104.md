# Literature novelty check for sub-journal directions (2026-11-04)

> Written by a subagent (web / PubMed / bioRxiv, mostly abstract level). Items marked UNVERIFIED, and any number quoted from another paper, still need checking against the original before use in the manuscript. Used by SCREEN_SUBJOURNAL.md.


## Cross-cutting flags (read first)
1. **GENCODE lncRNA jump is a single event.** Release table (gencodegenes.org/human/releases.html): v47 = 10.2024, v48 = 05.2025, v49 = 09.2025, **v50 = 06.2026 is the latest; no v51 listed.** lncRNA genes (general stats pages, stats_NN.html): v41 19,095; v46 20,310; **v47 35,934**; v49 35,899; v50 35,885. Biotype tables give lower counts (v41 18,041; v49 34,880), which probably explains your 17,960 / 34,677. The growth is the CLS long-read import (Kaur...Guigo, bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.10.29.620654; the GENCODE CLS page gives >132k new lncRNA transcripts in v47; I found no journal version). v47-v50 is flat.
2. **SpliceAI's own lncRNA numbers are high.** Miyachi & Nakai (NAR 2026;54:gkag625, doi:10.1093/nar/gkag625) re-evaluated SpliceAI on the lncRNA test set shipped with the SpliceAI paper (Jaganathan 2019, Cell, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.12.015). They report recall 0.944 (GENCODE protein-coding) vs 0.795 (lncRNA), but auPRC 0.945 vs 0.963. Your AP of 0.14 (motif-matched candidates, CLS-era sites) differs by design. You must bridge this by computing the SpliceAI-style top-k/auPRC on your own site sets.
3. **AlphaGenome is a mandatory baseline.** Its splice-site/junction labels come from STAR-aligned GTEx/ENCODE RNA-seq filtered by read counts, not from a biotype list (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.25.661532; Avsec et al., Nature 649:1206, 2026, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-10014-0). It has seen lncRNA junctions. I found no per-biotype evaluation (UNVERIFIED).
4. **Vipsania is a protein-coding gene finder** (Krieg...Stanke, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.08.26.747235). Its AP of 0.037 on lncRNA sites is out-of-scope by design. Frame it as a control, not a failure.
5. **Circularity for Snaptron-based negatives.** The CLS pipeline already required >=50 recount reads per intron (same preprint). Positives from v47+ are therefore recount-supported by construction. Define positives and negatives from recount3/Snaptron independently of GENCODE.

## D1 Locus-structure dissociation / splice-site predictability ceiling
- (a) Closest prior:
  - Biology: Mele 2017 (Genome Res 27:27, doi:10.1101/gr.214205.116: lincRNAs spliced less efficiently, weaker motifs, longer branchpoint-3'ss distance); Krchnakova 2019 (NAR 47(2), PMC6344860: lincRNA splicing efficiency tracks 5'ss strength, unlike expression-matched mRNAs, with fewer SR-protein binding sites); Tilgner 2012 (Genome Res 22:1616); Schlackow 2017 (Mol Cell, PMID 28017589: weak co-transcriptional splicing, early termination); Schuler/Hurst 2014 (MBE 31:3164, doi:10.1093/molbev/msu249: splice-related motif constraint in lincRNAs, not expression level).
  - Predictors: SpliceSelectNet (above, flag 2). The SpliceAI, Pangolin (Zeng & Li, Genome Biol 2022, doi:10.1186/s13059-022-02664-4), SpliceTransformer (You et al., Nat Commun 2024, doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53088-6), Splam (Genome Biol 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03379-4) and OpenSpliceAI (default biotype = protein-coding; eLife RP 107454) evaluations I found are protein-coding only.
- (b) **Partially novel.** "lncRNA splicing is weaker and less efficient, with expression-matched controls" is established biology. "Deep splice models perform worse on lncRNAs" is only hinted at (recall drop in SpliceSelectNet), and I found no systematic cross-model study. I found no expression-matched mRNA-vs-lncRNA predictor comparison, but I cannot rule one out.
- (c) New for you:
  - A multi-model ceiling (SpliceAI, Pangolin, SpliceTransformer, AlphaGenome, DNA LMs, your CNN) on one recount-defined positive/negative set.
  - Matching on expression and on annotation age (pre-v47 vs CLS-added).
  - A decomposition of the gap into expression, motif strength and annotation completeness.
  - The link to intron-chain Sn (0 to 0.071 with oracle sites).
- (d) Data: recount3 (Wilks 2021, Genome Biol, doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02533-6), human SRA ~316k samples / ~228M junctions via Snaptron (snaptron.cs.jhu.edu/data.html); GTEx junctions; GENCODE v41/v47/v50 GTFs.

## D2 Prospective validation
- (a) Closest prior:
  - EGASP (Guigo 2006, Genome Biol, doi:10.1186/gb-2006-7-s1-s2) assessed predictions against a reference set at one time point.
  - Jalali et al. 2016 (Hum Genomics, doi:10.1186/s40246-016-0090-2) tracks GENCODE v1-v24 churn but does not test predictions.
  - I found no study testing sequence-only predictions against later releases for lncRNA or for genes generally (UNVERIFIED completeness).
- (b) **Likely novel**, and the strongest "new conclusion" candidate.
- (c) Train on v41 (July 2022). Test whether the ~16.8k lncRNA genes added by v47 (Oct 2024, long-read, tissue-derived) are enriched among your calls, with positional controls. Caveats:
  - CLS probes were designed from GENCODE v27 plus other catalogs, so the targeting is annotation-biased.
  - Many v47 loci overlap LncBook/FANTOM, so report a "novel to all catalogs" stratum.
  - A formal forward test must wait for v51+; freeze and hash your predictions now (timestamped deposit). v47 to v50 added no lncRNA loci, so expect little from v51.
- (d) GENCODE v41/v47/v50 GTFs (gencodegenes.org); LncBook 2.0 (95,243 genes; NAR, PMC9825513; v2.1 released 2024-09).

## D3 Genome-wide / cross-species lncRNA atlas from sequence alone
- (a) Closest prior:
  - GENATATORs (Shmelev...Fishman, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.06.17.732686, 2026-06-21): human chr20 plus 39 mammals, with cross-species tests including Arabidopsis; "lncRNA detection lower in mammals" (Fig 4C).
  - SegmentNT (de Almeida et al., Nat Methods 22:2301, 2025, doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02881-2): 14 element classes including lncRNA, multispecies model.
  - NTv3 (Boshar et al., bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.22.695963): ab initio gene annotation, protein-coding focus. Whether it scores lncRNA is UNVERIFIED.
  - Helixer (Nat Methods 2025, doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02939-1) and Tiberius (Bioinformatics 2024, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae685): protein-coding.
  - Evo 2 (Nature 652:1349, 2026, doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5): exon/intron features; no lncRNA annotation found.
- (b) **Partially novel.** No released genome-wide human or cross-species lncRNA locus atlas from sequence alone was found. GENATATORs is the nearest.
- (c) A calibrated atlas with precision estimates and orthogonal support (RNA-seq, synteny) for non-model genomes.
- (d) Ensembl/NCBI genomes; matched RNA-seq (SRA) for validation species; chicken/zebrafish Ensembl annotations.

## D4 Positional conservation / synteny
- (a) Closest prior: Ulitsky 2011 (Cell); Necsulea 2014 (Nature, doi:10.1038/nature12943); Hezroni 2015 (Cell Rep 11:1110, doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2015.04.023); Nitsche 2015 (RNA 21:801, doi:10.1261/rna.046342.114: splice-site conservation, fast intron/exon turnover); the coPARSE-lncRNA synteny random forest (Nat Genet 56:124, 2024, doi:10.1038/s41588-023-01620-7); ortho2align. Necsulea 2014 and Washietl 2014 used sequence conservation first, then RNA-seq (ascertainment bias, per Hezroni). I found no case where a sequence-only predictor's lncRNA loci were validated by synteny.
- (b) **Partially novel** (a validation method that exists, applied to a new source of loci).
- (c) Use synteny/positional orthology as an RNA-seq-independent precision estimate. Locus-level conservation fits your result (locus yes, structure no), since Nitsche shows structures turn over.
- (d) Ensembl Compara or UCSC chains and liftOver; Hezroni 17-species lncRNA sets; the Sarropoulos 2019 (Nature) developmental lncRNA catalog.

The main text gives no lncRNA numbers (Appendix not read), so I cannot confirm whether it reports intron-chain accuracy. It compares against Tiberius and AlphaGenome and shows lncRNA segmentation harder than mRNA.

- No 2025-26 paper reporting lncRNA intron-chain accuracy from sequence alone was found.
- (b) **Partially novel.** Your locus-level advantage over GENATATORs (0.306 vs 0.028 at the same precision) and your honest negative intron-chain result are both citable differentiators. Verify GENATATORs' Appendix before claiming "first".
